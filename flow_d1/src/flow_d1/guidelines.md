# Guidelines for Mapping Dream Content to PTSD Trauma Cues

There are two main official guides for PTSD identification: **DSM-5** and **ICD-11**.  
Below are their criteria, structured to be machine-/agent-friendly. Following that: suggested rules for mapping dream descriptions to PTSD triggers/trauma cues.

---

## DSM-5 Criteria for PTSD

All of the following criteria (A through H) must be evaluated; some require ≥1, ≥2 symptoms, etc. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

| Criterion | Description |
|---|---|
| **Criterion A (Stressor)** | Exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence, in one (or more) of the following ways: direct experience; witnessing; learning that a close family member or friend was exposed; indirect exposure (e.g., as a first responder) :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1} |
| **Criterion B (Intrusion symptoms)** | Persistence of one (or more) intrusion symptoms associated with the traumatic event(s), beginning after the event(s): <br> • Unwanted, distressing memories <br> • Nightmares <br> • Flashbacks <br> • Intense or prolonged psychological distress at exposure to reminders <br> • Marked physiological reactions to reminders :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2} |
| **Criterion C (Avoidance)** | Persistent avoidance of stimuli associated with the traumatic event(s), beginning after the event(s): <br> • Avoidance of distressing trauma-related memories, thoughts, or feelings <br> • Avoidance of external reminders (people, places, conversations, activities, objects, situations) that arouse distressing memories, thoughts or feelings :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3} |
| **Criterion D (Negative alterations in cognitions & mood)** | Two (or more) of the following, beginning or worsening after the traumatic event(s): <br> • Inability to remember important aspects of the trauma (amnesia) <br> • Persistent and exaggerated negative beliefs/expectations about self, others, or the world <br> • Distorted blame of self or others for causing the trauma <br> • Persistent negative emotional state (fear, horror, anger, guilt, shame, etc.) <br> • Markedly diminished interest or participation in significant activities <br> • Feelings of detachment or estrangement from others <br> • Inability to experience positive emotions (happiness, love, satisfaction) :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4} |
| **Criterion E (Alterations in arousal & reactivity)** | Two (or more) of the following, beginning or worsening after the traumatic event(s): <br> • Irritable behavior / angry outbursts <br> • Reckless or self-destructive behavior <br> • Hypervigilance <br> • Exaggerated startle response <br> • Problems with concentration <br> • Sleep disturbance (difficulty falling/staying asleep, restless sleep) :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5} |
| **Criterion F (Duration)** | Duration of the disturbance (symptoms in B, C, D, E) is more than one month :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6} |
| **Criterion G (Functional significance)** | The disturbance causes clinically significant distress or impairment in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7} |
| **Criterion H (Exclusion)** | The disturbance is not attributable to the physiological effects of a substance (e.g., medication, alcohol) or another medical condition :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8} |

**Specifications / Subtypes**  
- Dissociative subtype: depersonalization or derealization in reaction to trauma-related stimuli. :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}  
- Delayed expression: full criteria not met until at least 6 months after the trauma(s), though onset of symptoms may occur earlier. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}  
- PTSD preschool subtype for children ≤6 years (with somewhat modified symptom expression). :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

## ICD-11 Criteria for PTSD (and related: Complex PTSD)

ICD-11 defines PTSD more narrowly; also introduces **Complex PTSD (CPTSD)** for more severe, prolonged, or relationally rooted trauma. :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| **Exposure / Stressor Guidance** | PTSD (or CPTSD) *may* develop following exposure to an **extremely threatening or horrific event or series of events**. ICD-11 does **not** have a fully formal “Criterion A” (exact definition) like DSM-5, but the exposure guidance is required context for diagnosis. :contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13} |
| **Essential (Core) Features of PTSD** | To diagnose PTSD, the following must be present: <br> 1. **Re-experiencing the traumatic event(s) in the present** (e.g. vivid intrusive memories, day-time flashbacks, nightmares) <br> 2. **Avoidance** of traumatic reminders (internal or external) <br> 3. **Sense of current threat** — hypervigilance, exaggerated startle response, etc. <br> 4. **Functional impairment** in important areas of life (social, occupational, etc.) <br> 5. **Duration**: symptoms lasting for several weeks after the event. :contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14} |
| **Complex PTSD (CPTSD) Additional Features** | Beyond PTSD core, CPTSD requires disturbances in self-organization (DSO), which include: <br> - Affective dysregulation (difficulty regulating emotions, both hyper‐ and hypoactivation) <br> - Negative self-concept (persistent beliefs about self as diminished, defeated, or worthless; feelings of shame, guilt, failure) <br> - Interpersonal difficulties (difficulty in maintaining relationships or feeling close to others) :contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15} |
| **Associated & Clinical Features** | These are common but not required; they help in recognizing variant presentations. Include somatic complaints, altered somatic perceptions; co-occurring depression, anxiety, substance use; shame, guilt, etc. :contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16} |
| **Developmental / Age Considerations** | In children/adolescents, symptom manifestation may differ; certain trauma types (chronic, relational) often lead to CPTSD rather than simpler PTSD. Also cognitive/emotional developmental stage affects symptom forms. :contentReference[oaicite:17]{index=17} |

## Comparison: DSM-5 vs ICD-11 (for PTSD)

| Difference | DSM-5 Strengths / Features | ICD-11 Strengths / Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Number & breadth of symptoms** | More symptoms: intrusion, avoidance, negative mood/cognitions, arousal/reactivity; broad set in D & E. | Narrower core symptom set: focuses on re-experiencing, avoidance, current threat. Reduces overlap with other disorders. :contentReference[oaicite:18]{index=18} |
| **Formal vs. guidance for exposure** | DSM-5 has explicit Criterion A. | ICD-11 uses guidance for exposure (extremely threatening/horrific), but less rigid. |
| **Functional impairment requirement** | DSM-5: Criterion G explicitly. | ICD-11: required for both PTSD and more so for CPTSD. |
| **Handling prolonged/chronic trauma** | DSM-5 includes a broad range of trauma types; complex symptoms often included under broader PTSD criteria or comorbid diagnoses. | ICD-11 introduces CPTSD for disturbances beyond core PTSD when trauma is prolonged/multiple, especially relational/interpersonal. |

## How to Use These Criteria to Map Dream Descriptions → Possible Trauma Cues

Below is a suggested **guideline** for an AI agent to map content in a dream to likely PTSD-relevant triggers / cues, using the criteria above.

### Guidelines / Rules

1. **Extract dream features**  
   - Entities: people, places, objects, sensory modalities (sight, sound, smell, touch, movement).  
   - Emotions: fear, shame, guilt, helplessness, horror, anger, sorrow, etc.  
   - Physical sensations: heart racing, suffocation, falling, etc.  
   - Temporal / spatial cues: recurrent, flashbacks, distortions of time/space.  
   - Reminders or symbolic parallels: e.g., being chased, enclosed, interpersonal betrayal, etc.

2. **Match to DSM-5 criteria**  
   For each extracted dream feature, ask: Does this correspond to / suggest:  
   - Intrusion (memories, flashbacks, nightmares) → nightmares or sudden reliving in dream?  
   - Avoidance cues → dream avoidance, or avoidance content in dream?  
   - Negative cognitions / mood: guilt, shame, self-blame, negative beliefs.  
   - Arousal / reactivity: being hypervigilant in dream, startled, difficulty sleeping.  
   - Duration & impairment: is it recurring, frequent?

3. **Match to ICD-11 criteria**  
   Does dream content suggest:  
   - Re-experiencing in the here-and-now (vividness, models of flashback/nightmare)?  
   - Avoidance of reminders (dream includes reminders, or content about avoiding something)?  
   - Sense of current threat (being under threat even though event is past—in dream)**?  
   - Functional impact: recurring dreams that affect waking life (sleep, mood).  
   - For CPTSD: additional themes: self-worth, relational distrust or isolation, emotional dysregulation.

4. **Weighting / scoring**  
   One possible structure: assign “points” for features matched:  
   | Criterion matched | Points |
   |---|---|
   | DSM-5: B / ICD-11 re-experiencing | 2 |
   | DSM-5: Avoidance / ICD-11 avoidance | 2 |
   | DSM-5: Negative mood / cognition / self-concept / shame etc. | 1 |
   | DSM-5: Arousal / current threat | 2 |
   | Recurrence / impairment / duration | 1 |
   | CPTSD extra features (if present) | additional bonus |
